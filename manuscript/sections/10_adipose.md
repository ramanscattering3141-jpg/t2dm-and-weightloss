## 10. Adipose tissue, body composition and metabolic adaptation

**Composition of weight lost.** In a DXA substudy of SURMOUNT-1 (no diabetes), tirzepatide reduced weight by 21.3%, fat mass by 33.9% and lean mass by 10.9%. About three-quarters of the weight lost was fat in both drug and placebo groups [@LOOK2025]. In T2D, semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced fat mass by 3.4 kg and lean mass by 2.3 kg over 52 weeks in a SUSTAIN 8 substudy, but there was no placebo arm and the dose was lower [@MCCRIMMON2020]. Tirzepatide reduced liver fat and visceral and subcutaneous adipose volumes compared with insulin degludec [@GASTALDELLI2022]. Semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced visceral fat area by 40% in an East Asian population with and without T2D [@STEP6]. No study has compared body-composition changes between populations with and without T2D at matched dose and duration.

**Body composition cannot easily explain smaller weight loss.** Losing a kilogram of fat removes about 39.5 MJ of stored energy, compared with about 7.6 MJ for a kilogram of lean tissue [@HALL2011]. If people with T2D lost a larger share of weight as lean tissue, a given energy deficit would produce *more* weight loss, not less. A composition difference could reduce weight loss only through fluid or glycogen shifts (Section 8.2). Those are short-term effects and have not been measured in this context.

**Energy expenditure and adaptation.** In humans, incretin agonists do not increase energy expenditure. Semaglutide did not change resting metabolic rate adjusted for lean mass [@BLUNDELL2017]. Liraglutide did not change 24-hour expenditure in T2D [@HARDER2004] and transiently *reduced* resting expenditure in a 26-week T2D trial [@VANEYK2019]. Tirzepatide did not change metabolic adaptation in obesity but increased fat oxidation [@RAVUSSIN2025]. The GLP-1/glucagon co-agonist cotadutide produced weight loss in T2D driven mainly by reduced intake, with little change in expenditure [@GOLUBIC2024]. One adaptation is specific to diabetes. Resting metabolic rate is about 5% higher in T2D than in equally obese people with normal glucose tolerance, and it falls as glycaemia is corrected [@BOGARDUS1986; @MAKIMATTILA1999]. Normalising glycaemia thus removes an energy cost unique to the diabetic state, adding to the glycosuric offset in Section 9.

**Physical activity** has not been measured objectively and compared between populations in incretin trials. Whether lower activity in older, more comorbid T2D populations contributes is unknown.
