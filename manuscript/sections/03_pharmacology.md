## 3. Incretin pharmacology: separating glucose lowering from weight loss

### 3.1 Endogenous physiology versus pharmacology

Endogenous GLP-1 and GIP are short-lived peptides secreted after meals. Both potentiate glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. GLP-1 also suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying and reduces food intake [@DRUCKER_HOLST2023]. In T2D the incretin effect, the extra insulin secreted after oral compared with intravenous glucose at matched glycaemia, is markedly reduced: incretin factors accounted for 36% of the insulin response in T2D versus 73% in matched controls [@NAUCK1986]. The defect is largely a consequence of the diabetic state rather than its cause. It appears in diabetes secondary to chronic pancreatitis but not in pancreatitis with normal glucose tolerance [@KNOP2007]. Four weeks of near-normoglycaemia improved the late-phase β-cell response to GLP-1 and GIP three- to fourfold [@HOJBERG2009].

These findings concern the endocrine pancreas at physiological hormone concentrations. They do not imply reduced responsiveness to long-acting agonists at pharmacological exposures. Infused GLP-1 retained most of its insulinotropic activity in mild T2D, whereas the insulinotropic effect of GIP was reduced by 54% [@NAUCK1993]. GIP also failed to amplify late-phase secretion [@VILSBOLL2002]. Graded GLP-1 infusions increased β-cell glucose responsiveness in T2D, but at each dose the response was 3–5 times smaller than in controls. Even so, a low dose restored responsiveness to the level of untreated controls [@KJEMS2003]. A reduced physiological incretin effect is therefore a statement about the islet. It says nothing direct about the brain and gut circuits through which agonists reduce energy intake.

### 3.2 Two outputs, partly separate pathways

The glucose-lowering effects of incretin drugs arise mainly from islet mechanisms (insulin secretion and glucagon suppression), from slower gastric emptying of meal glucose, and secondarily from weight reduction and improved insulin sensitivity. In a mechanistic study in T2D, tirzepatide improved the clamp disposition index, total insulin secretion, insulin sensitivity (M value) and glucagon suppression more than semaglutide 1 mg [@HEISE2022]. Weight reduction, by contrast, depends on a sustained negative energy balance. That balance is driven mainly by reduced energy intake, with little or no increase in energy expenditure in humans (Sections 4 and 10). Both outputs share the receptor but not the effector organs. This is why a drug can improve glycaemia without changing weight, as low-dose liraglutide (0.6 mg) did in an 8-week respiratory-chamber study in T2D [@HARDER2004], and why glycaemic correction can feed back on energy balance (Section 9).

### 3.3 Exposure, receptor engagement and molecular class

Long-acting peptide agonists achieve sustained plasma concentrations far above endogenous GLP-1. Within T2D, semaglutide exposure varies mainly with body weight [@CARLSSON2018]. Oral and subcutaneous formulations show nearly identical exposure–response relationships for HbA1c and body weight, which suggests that variation between individuals in GLP-1 receptor responsiveness is not a major determinant of response [@OVERGAARD2021]. Agents differ in other ways. Tirzepatide engages the GIP receptor more than the GLP-1 receptor at clinical doses, and at the GLP-1 receptor it favours cAMP signalling over β-arrestin recruitment and causes less receptor internalisation [@WILLARD2020]. Orforglipron is a non-peptide oral agonist [@ATTAIN1]. Cagrilintide adds amylin receptor agonism [@REDEFINE1]. Maridebart cafraglutide pairs GLP-1 agonism with GIP receptor antagonism [@MARITIDE2025]. Despite these differences, the attenuation ratio in T2D is similar across agents (Table 1). That similarity is the first indication that the explanation lies mainly in the host rather than in a receptor-specific pharmacodynamic defect.
