## 7. GIP biology and dual receptor agonism

### 7.1 Established human pharmacology

The insulinotropic action of GIP is reduced in T2D, both in peak effect [@NAUCK1993] and in amplifying late-phase secretion [@VILSBOLL2002]. Near-normalisation of glycaemia partly restores it [@HOJBERG2009]. In rat islets, chronic hyperglycaemia reduces expression of both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, and normalising glucose reverses the change [@XU2007]. This finding suggests a mechanism, but it is animal data. GIP has adipose actions in humans. Together with hyperinsulinaemia it increased subcutaneous adipose blood flow, glucose uptake, triglyceride hydrolysis and fatty-acid re-esterification [@ASMAR2010]. Infused GIP did not reduce energy intake in overweight men, and adding GIP to GLP-1 did not enhance the GLP-1-induced reduction in intake [@BERGMANN2019].

### 7.2 Mechanisms proposed mainly from animal and cellular studies

In mice, hypothalamic cells expressing the GIP receptor suppress food intake when activated [@ADRIAENSSENS2019]. Central GIP receptors are required for the anorectic effect of acyl-GIP and for the greater efficacy of GLP-1/GIP co-agonism over GLP-1 alone [@ZHANG2021]. In mice, rats and shrews, GIP receptor agonism attenuates GLP-1-induced emesis while preserving reduced food intake [@BORNER2021]. Paradoxically, GIP receptor antagonist antibodies also reduce weight and enhance GLP-1 agonist effects in rodents and non-human primates [@KILLION2018]. One explanation is that sustained agonism produces functional antagonism through receptor desensitisation, but this remains unresolved. At the receptor level, tirzepatide is biased in its signalling and engages the GIP receptor more than the GLP-1 receptor [@WILLARD2020].

### 7.3 Does altered GIP physiology in T2D predict a different response to dual agonism?

If reduced GIP receptor responsiveness in T2D blunted the extra weight loss attributable to GIP agonism, the ratio of T2D to non-T2D weight loss should be lower for tirzepatide than for selective GLP-1 receptor agonists. It is not: the ratio was 0.59–0.65 for tirzepatide and 0.50–0.60 for semaglutide (Table 1). The attenuation is also present with maridebart cafraglutide, which *antagonises* the GIP receptor (ratio about 0.77) [@MARITIDE2025], and with orforglipron, which has no GIP activity [@ATTAIN2]. The weight deficit in T2D therefore does not depend on the direction of GIP receptor pharmacology. This makes impaired GIP signalling an unlikely primary explanation.

The mechanistic data in T2D point instead to effects of tirzepatide beyond intake. In T2D, tirzepatide produced more weight and fat-mass loss than semaglutide 1 mg without a measurable difference in ad libitum intake [@HEISE2023]. It also produced larger improvements in insulin sensitivity [@HEISE2022]. In people with obesity but without diabetes, tirzepatide increased fat oxidation without changing metabolic adaptation [@RAVUSSIN2025]. Larger weight loss with dual agonism does not prove a specific GIP-mediated anorectic mechanism in humans. The relative contributions of greater GLP-1 receptor exposure, GIP-mediated tolerability, adipose effects and substrate effects are not established.
