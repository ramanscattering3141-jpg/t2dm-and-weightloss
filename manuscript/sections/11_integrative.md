## 11. Integrative model: from diabetes pathophysiology to weight-loss response

Figure 2 organises the evidence into four interacting domains. Each causal link is labelled by the strength of its support. Table 3 summarises the evidence for each candidate mechanism.

**Drug exposure and pharmacodynamics.** Dose, duration and estimand were matched in the comparisons. Exposure depends mainly on body weight, and tolerability and discontinuation do not favour the trials without diabetes. Receptor pharmacology varies widely across agents that all show the deficit, including opposite directions of GIP receptor action. Reduced drug exposure or a receptor-specific defect is therefore unlikely to be a primary determinant.

**Energy intake.** Intake suppression is the main mechanism of incretin-induced weight loss in both populations and is preserved, at least qualitatively, in T2D. Its magnitude has never been compared between populations under matched conditions. A smaller central or gastrointestinal effect in T2D, through hypothalamic gliosis, central insulin resistance, greater food-cue reactivity or altered baseline gastric emptying, is biologically plausible but unmeasured. This is the largest unknown in the model.

**Peripheral incretin signalling.** Upstream of the brain sits a secretory, degradative and afferent apparatus that diabetes measurably alters (Section 5). The gut–vagal glucose-sensing pathway degrades in type 2 diabetic and insulin-resistant animals, intact vagal innervation is required for native GLP-1 to suppress intake in humans, and agonist-induced anorexia is blunted in a dysmetabolic rodent host with nodose receptor downregulation. Three findings bound the contribution: incretin secretion is on average preserved in T2D and falls only with worsening glycaemic control; the portal afferent receptor appears not to be engaged by exogenous agonists; and prolonging native GLP-1 with DPP-4 inhibition does not reduce weight. The most defensible version of this mechanism is therefore not pharmacological resistance but the loss of a physiological, paracrine satiation signal that normally acts alongside the drug — graded with glycaemic control, and never measured in either population on treatment.

**Energy expenditure and substrate metabolism.** Agonists do not raise expenditure, and adaptive changes appear similar in kind across populations. Correcting hyperglycaemia removes the excess resting metabolic rate seen in T2D. Changes in substrate partitioning and insulin sensitivity alter fuel selection, but there is no evidence that they set the size of the energy deficit.

**Energy losses and glycaemic correction.** Elimination of glycosuria returns previously lost glucose energy to the body, and correcting hyperglycaemia removes the excess resting metabolic rate of the diabetic state. The size of both effects is established in diabetes generally. A formal energy-balance model, fitted to non-diabetic semaglutide response and then applied to T2D with appetite parameters held fixed, partially reproduces the observed gap from these two terms alone, and predicts that stable background SGLT2 inhibition should produce the greatest attenuation [@HALL2026]. Our independent scenario arithmetic reaches a compatible magnitude. This is now the best-supported mechanism in the model. Neither component has been measured in an incretin trial.

**How several modest effects combine.** Together, the energy offsets from glycaemic correction (glycosuria and metabolic rate) and weight-promoting co-therapies could narrow the net energy deficit in the drug arm. Because weight approaches a new steady state over a year or more, small persistent offsets matter: each 24 kcal/day of persistent offset corresponds to about 1 kg at steady state [@HALL2011]. Population differences in sex and age, and possibly behaviour [@WING1987; @RODRIGUEZ2024], add further differences between trials. We deliberately do not assign percentage contributions. None of these components has been measured in both populations under the same treatment, so any apportionment would be speculative.

### 11.1 Three host-level hypotheses, and what would separate them

The cross-intervention evidence (Section 2.5) establishes that the operative mechanism is in the host, not in incretin receptor pharmacology. It does not say which host mechanism. Three remain live, they are not mutually exclusive, and the phenotype as currently described is compatible with all three:

* **H1 — Energetics.** Correcting hyperglycaemia returns glycosuric energy and removes the excess resting metabolic rate of the diabetic state, narrowing the net deficit in the drug arm (Section 9).
* **H2 — Generalised anorectic-responsiveness deficit.** The diabetic brain responds less to satiation signalling of any kind, so a given pharmacological or surgical stimulus produces less intake suppression (Section 4.3).
* **H3 — Degraded shared afferent machinery.** The multi-axis gut–brain pathway that carries CCK, PYY, ghrelin, distension and incretin signals alike is damaged, blunting transmission regardless of which signal is amplified (Section 5).

The table below sets out where they diverge. The essential point is that almost every observation currently in hand is predicted by all three; only two rows discriminate, and both are thinly populated.

Two rows carry the weight. The **prediabetes step** favours H2 and H3: roughly 2 percentage points of deficit have appeared where there is almost no glycosuria to retain, and the central and afferent abnormalities are documented in impaired glucose tolerance [@ROSENBAUM2022; @LEE2012]. The **fixed-intake experiment** favours H1: when intake was enforced at 300 kcal/day, the gap persisted [@HENRY1986] — though with a baseline composition confounder, and against a meta-analytic null [@LESLIE2017]. These two rows point in opposite directions, which is the honest summary of the field.

One methodological point deserves emphasis because it is easy to misread. The modelling analysis of Section 9.3 held the appetite parameters **fixed** at their non-diabetic values [@HALL2026]. That is what makes its energetic attribution clean, but it also means the model was never allowed to express H2. A blunted appetite response in T2D could not appear as a fitted parameter; it could only appear as residual unexplained gap — which is exactly what the analysis reports. The model therefore demonstrates that energetics can account for *part* of the gap. It cannot distinguish "energetics explains part and nothing else is needed" from "energetics explains part and blunted anorectic response explains the rest," because the second possibility was excluded by construction. Refitting the appetite parameters in T2D and reporting how much they must change would be a direct and inexpensive test.

**The single most discriminating measurement** is therefore energy intake. If placebo-adjusted intake suppression is equal in both populations on the same agent and the weight gap persists, H1 is right and H2 is wrong. If intake suppression is smaller in T2D, H2 or H3 is operating and energetics alone cannot be the answer. No study has made this comparison (Section 4.2), which is why it is the first study in Table 4.

**Which explanations are best supported?** Ranked by evidence:

* **Best supported (the phenotype itself).** The gap exists, is graded with glycaemic status, is confined to the drug-attributable component, and is not specific to incretin drugs — it appears with unrelated pharmacotherapies and after bariatric surgery. Drug exposure and tolerability do not explain it. The mechanism is therefore in the host.
* **Best *quantified*, and the only one with a formal model: energetics (H1).** Glycosuric energy retention and the fall in resting expenditure are established in other settings, are quantitatively capable of a substantial share, and partially reproduce the gap in simulation [@HALL2026]. This is the best-*characterised* hypothesis, which is not the same as the best-*supported*: its mediation has never been measured, it cannot explain the prediabetes step, and the model that supports it excluded the main competing hypothesis by construction.
* **Equally compatible with the evidence, far less investigated: blunted anorectic responsiveness (H2) and degraded shared afferent machinery (H3).** Both predict everything H1 predicts, plus the prediabetes step that H1 cannot. Both have direct human mechanistic support — failed hypothalamic inhibition after glucose in BMI-matched T2D [@VIDARSDOTTIR2007], gliosis in IGT and T2D [@ROSENBAUM2022], loss of GLP-1's intake effect after human vagotomy [@PLAMBOECK2013] — and neither has been tested as a mediator of the weight gap. Their lower ranking in earlier drafts reflected the absence of a quantitative model, not the weight of evidence against them.
* **Plausible and quantitatively capable, but unmeasured.** Weight-promoting background therapy. Background SGLT2 inhibition. Population composition by sex and age.
* **Weakest.** An intrinsic, insulin-resistance-driven pharmacological resistance. Impaired GIP signalling as the primary cause. A reduced endogenous incretin *secretory* deficit as the primary cause. Note these are weak as *primary* explanations; the broad, multi-axis version of the afferent hypothesis (H3) is not among them.
