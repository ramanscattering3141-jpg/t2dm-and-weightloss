## 12. Unresolved questions and future directions

The central gap is that no study has measured the components of energy balance in people with and without T2D receiving the same incretin agent. A formal modelling analysis has now shown that the energetic offsets of glycaemic correction are capable of partially explaining the gap, and has generated specific, falsifiable predictions [@HALL2026] — but a model that reproduces an observation is not evidence that the modelled mechanism produced it, since competing mechanisms may fit the same trajectories equally well. Direct measurement remains the decisive step, and the author of that analysis says so. Table 4 sets out studies that would resolve the main uncertainties, in order of priority.

Several design principles apply across these studies:

* **Mediation analyses.** Studies should prespecify mediation analyses that partition the diabetes difference into intake, expenditure and urinary-loss components, rather than reporting each in isolation. Because expenditure is itself a determinant of intake [@BLUNDELL2020; @HOPKINS2023], these components must be modelled as a coupled system rather than as additive terms, and a measured change in intake cannot be read directly as evidence of a primary appetite defect (Section 9.5).
* **Measure compensation, do not assume it.** The single largest uncertainty in the energetic account is what fraction of the retained energy is offset by a change in intake. Compensation reaches high fractions by about 80 weeks in exercise interventions [@RIOU2015; @DOUCET2018], but whether that transfers to an energy leak being closed, under pharmacological appetite suppression, is unknown and may differ sharply. Serial doubly labelled water with repeated intake measurement over the full 68–72 weeks, rather than at a single timepoint, is required to estimate it.
* **Controlled feeding.** Short controlled-feeding studies under matched agonist exposure could separate drug effects on intake from effects on energy disposal.
* **Measurement in T2D trials.** Phase 3 programmes in T2D could add urinary glucose collection and expenditure measurement at low cost, which would make these data routine. Baseline autonomic reflex testing and a stored meal-stimulated incretin sample would add similarly little burden and would make studies 7 and 8 answerable retrospectively.
* **Separating pathway from host.** Studies 7 and 9 test the peripheral hypothesis of Section 5 in the only way available in humans: by varying vagal integrity rather than diabetes status. Because vagal dysfunction and hyperglycaemia co-vary, either design needs adjustment for HbA1c, and a null result in study 9 would locate the agonist's anorectic effect centrally and largely close the question.
